The three jobs

Most lots need four contractors. We are one.

Hiring a grader, a plumber, an electrician and a set crew separately is where schedules fall apart and nobody owns the gap between them.

Raw lot to keys, in five moves.

Walk the lot

Soil, slope, setbacks, and where the utilities actually stub out. Free, and usually an hour.

Plans and permits

Engineered pad detail drawn, county submittal filed, inspection dates on the calendar.

Dirt work

Clear, cut, fill, compact, test. Driveway and drainage go in while the machines are on site.

Utilities in

Trench, lay, pressure test, inspect, backfill. Nothing gets covered before it is signed.

Set and tie down

Placed, blocked, leveled, anchored, marriage line closed, skirting and final grade.

A manufactured home arrives on a steel frame, moves with the ground, and gets inspected against HUD rules, not framing rules.

On a clean lot with utilities at the road, plan on two to three weeks of field work. Permit review is the variable. Rural lots needing a well, septic and a long power run stretch that out, and we tell you which one is the long pole before you sign.

Yes. Plenty of our jobs are pad only, with the dealer's crew doing the set. We coordinate dates so the pad is finished and tested before the transport shows up.

If a sewer main runs past your frontage you tie in, and that is almost always cheaper. Outside a district you are on septic, which means a percolation test and a county design first. We check both on the walk and price the one you actually need.
